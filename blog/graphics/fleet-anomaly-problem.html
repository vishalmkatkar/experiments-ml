FLEET ANOMALY DETECTION · A 6-PART SERIES

An anomaly is not a failure

Most anomaly detection stops at a score. The questions that matter start after the alert.

One machine, three weeks, one fleet-wide alert thresholdIllustrative

Every startup crosses the line. So does a sensor glitch. The slow fault that ends in a failure looks just like the rest.

Most alerts are mode changes Startups, peak shifts and harder-working machines look unusual against one fleet-wide normal.
Failures are rare and logged late A handful per machine per year, timestamped when someone noticed, not when the fault began.
What fixed it is buried in free text Service notes say what worked, in different words from every technician.
Is this unusual?→What is going wrong?→What should we do? Most systems answer only the first question.
